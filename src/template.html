301 Darts Scorer · STM32 Breadboard Prototype · brief v4.8

Darts Scorer, 1 to 4 Players Blue Pill, I2C displays and LCDs, MAX7219 Cricket matrices, keypad, PWM audio, 5 V adapter

An STM32F103C8T6 Blue Pill scores 301, 501 or Cricket for one to four players. Two HT16K33 backpacks show the scores, two I2C LCDs show player text, two chains of eight MAX7219 8×8 matrices show Cricket marks, a 4×4 keypad takes input, and a PAM8403 amplifier plays the callouts with a switched 3.5 mm line output. A 5 V adapter on a barrel jack powers the board.

This page is the bring-up sequence. It walks 34 steps in nine stages, one subsystem at a time, and lights the exact pins for the step in progress. Each stage ends in a measured pass criterion before the next subsystem is connected.

    Design traps in this build
    

      

        
Matrix current sets the supply

        
One MAX7219 draws up to 330 mA with every LED on at 40 mA per segment. Sixteen of them can
        draw about 5.3 A at full intensity. Firmware caps the intensity register at 0x05 (11/32 duty),
        which brings the worst case to about 1.8 A for the matrices and about 2.3 A for the board. Use a
        regulated 5 V adapter rated 4 A or more, and raise the cap only after the module current is measured.

        
16 × 330 mA × 11/32 ≈ 1.8 A

      

      

        
Display-test mode ignores the cap

        
MAX7219 register 0x0F lights every LED at 31/32 duty whatever the intensity register says.
        It is used once, on a single module, to measure the worst case. On a full chain it draws about
        2.6 A. The chain test never calls it.

      

      

        
Matrix power stays off the breadboard

        
Each chain gets its own 22 AWG pair from TB1 and a 470 to 1000 µF capacitor at its input.
        Breadboard rails and dupont jumpers are not rated for a chain's current, and the drop along them
        starves the far modules. Pass is 4.75 V or more at module 7.

      

      

        
HCT, not HC

        
The MAX7219 needs a logic high of at least 3.5 V and the Blue Pill drives 3.3 V. The 74HCT541
        has a TTL-compatible input threshold near 2 V, so it reads a 3.3 V high correctly and outputs a full
        5 V level. A plain 74HC541 powered from 5 V needs about 3.5 V for a guaranteed high, which the MCU
        does not supply. Read the marking on the part before seating it.

      

      

        
The I2C bus runs at 5 V logic

        
The PCF8574 on each LCD backpack needs a high of at least 0.7 × VCC, which is 3.5 V on its
        5 V supply. A bus pulled to 3.3 V does not meet it. Both HT16K33 backpacks therefore run VIO from
        5 V, and every pull-up goes to 5 V. PB6 and PB7 are 5 V tolerant and drive open-drain. Never pull
        the bus to 3.3 V.

      

      

        
D6 isolates the adapter from USB

        
The 5 V rail feeds the Blue Pill 5V pin through D6, a 1N5819. With a USB cable plugged in for
        data, the computer holds VBUS above the diode cathode and the adapter cannot back-feed the
        computer. Never join the 5 V rail to the Blue Pill 5V pin directly.

      

      

        
Two 3.3 V regulators, never joined

        
The regulator on the Blue Pill supplies the MCU. The AMS1117 module supplies the 3.3 V rail for
        the ESP32 header. The Blue Pill 3V3 pin stays off the 3.3 V rail and feeds the brightness pot only.
        The ST-Link carries SWDIO, SWCLK and GND, and its 3.3 V pin stays open.

      

      

        
I2C address collisions

        
Both seven-segment backpacks ship at 0x70, and both LCD backpacks ship at one shared default.
        Bridge A0 on display 2 and on LCD B before they join the bus. Two devices at one address both
        acknowledge, and the scan cannot tell them apart.

      

      

        
Shared CLK and DIN on the matrix chains

        
The MAX7219 shifts data on every CLK edge whatever its LOAD line is doing. The idle chain shifts
        garbage but never latches it, because its LOAD stays high. Every write therefore covers all eight
        modules of one chain, which the driver always does.

      

      

        
PAM8403 outputs are bridge-tied

        
Each speaker terminal is driven. Neither output terminal connects to ground, so the 3.5 mm jack
        takes its signal before the amplifier. A jack sleeve on an amplifier output would short the
        negative half. The mono source is split at the input through two 10 kΩ resistors.

      

      

        
Amplifier and speaker ratings differ

        
The PAM8403 is rated 3 W per channel and the speaker 0.5 W. Firmware caps the PWM duty so the
        speaker is never driven past its rating. Volume is a firmware scale factor.

      

      

        
Audio filter corner

        
fc = 1 / (2πRC) = 1 / (2π × 1 kΩ × 0.1 µF) ≈ 1.6 kHz

        
Starting values. The coupling capacitor after the filter must be 0.1 to 1 µF. A capacitor
        marked 102 is 1000 pF, which passes almost no audio.

    If something is wrong
    

      
ST-Link cannot connect

      
Check SWDIO and SWCLK are not swapped, GND is common, and the ST-Link 3.3 V pin is open.
      If firmware set SYS Debug to No Debug, hold reset while connecting, then reflash with Serial Wire enabled.

      
Blue Pill does not power up from the adapter

      
D6 is reversed. The band goes toward the Blue Pill 5V pin.

      
I2C scan finds nothing

      
Check SDA is on PB7 and SCL on PB6, not reversed. Measure both lines idle: each must sit near the 5 V rail.
      A line stuck low points to a short or a reversed module supply. Confirm the scan passes the address shifted left by one bit.

      
Scan finds fewer addresses than devices

      
An A0 jumper is not bridged, so two devices share one address. Check display 2 and LCD B.

      
LCD backlight on, no text

      
Turn the backpack trim pot through its full range first. If text still never appears but
      LCDI2C_Backlight switches the backlight, the backpack bit map differs from the defines in lcd_i2c.c.

      
LCD shows a row of solid blocks

      
Power and contrast are correct and the controller is not initialized. Check the address in board.h matches the scan.

      
Matrices stay dark

      
Check CLK and DIN are not swapped at the module input, and that the module is wired at its input
      header, not its output header. Confirm CubeMX shows PB14 as GPIO_Output, not SPI2_MISO.

      
Module order is reversed or shifted

      
Module 0 must be the module wired to the buffer. If the pattern is mirrored, the chain is fed from the wrong end.

      
Far modules dim or flicker

      
The 5 V rail sags along the chain. Measure at module 7, shorten or thicken the power pair, and check the bulk capacitor polarity.

      
Keypad column 3, column 4 or row 4 never responds

      
PB3, PB4 and PA15 are JTAG pins at reset. They become GPIO only with SYS Debug set to Serial Wire.

      
Several keys register per press

      
Row and column leads are assigned to the wrong connector pins. Map the keypad connector with a
      continuity meter before trusting any published pin order.

      
Green or blue LED channel is dim

      
Their forward voltage is close to the 3.3 V pin drive level. Reduce those two resistors to 100 to 150 Ω. Leave red unchanged.

      
Audio is silent or very faint

      
Check the coupling capacitor value, then confirm PWM on PB8 with a meter on AC volts. Confirm the
      jack tip switch is closed with no plug, the amplifier has 5 V, and no speaker terminal touches ground.

      
Speaker keeps playing with a plug in

      
The amplifier is fed from the tip terminal instead of the tip switch terminal. Re-identify the jack terminals by continuity.

      
MCU resets when the matrices light

      
The adapter is undersized or the fuse is near trip. Measure total current, lower the intensity cap, and check the adapter rating.
